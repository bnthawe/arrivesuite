\# §3 Target Market \& Personas



\## 3.1 Launch Market



\*\*Primary:\*\* Botswana (BWP, English + Setswana)



Botswana is uniquely positioned as the launch market because:



\- \*\*Small and digitizable.\*\* \~2,000 BTO-licensed tourism products nationally. A focused team can onboard them all within 24 months.

\- \*\*Distinctive inventory.\*\* Okavango Delta, Chobe, Kalahari, Makgadikgadi, cultural tourism, MICE. Not replicable elsewhere.

\- \*\*Digital greenfield.\*\* No dominant local OTA. No entrenched competitor. Mobile money now common. BTO licensing digital-first.

\- \*\*Gateway to SADC.\*\* Once operational in Botswana, the same infrastructure extends to Namibia, Zambia, Zimbabwe, and South Africa.



\*\*Secondary (Phase 4+):\*\* Namibia · Zambia · Zimbabwe · South Africa



\*\*Tertiary (Phase 5+):\*\* International source markets (US, EU, UK, China, India, GCC)



\## 3.2 Product Market Fit by Persona



ArriveSuite serves six distinct personas. Each has different needs, and the platform addresses each differently.



\## 3.3 Persona P1 — Botswana Tourism Operator (Critical Persona)



This is the \*\*most important persona\*\* in the entire platform. Every other persona depends on this one.



\*\*Profile:\*\*



\- Lodge owner, guesthouse manager, tour operator, activity provider, or camp manager

\- Located in Maun, Kasane, Gaborone, Okavango, Chobe, Kalahari, or Makgadikgadi

\- Business size: 4–50 rooms or 1–20 tours per day

\- May have 3–50 staff

\- Uses WhatsApp as primary business communication

\- Manages bookings via paper calendar, WhatsApp, or a spreadsheet

\- May have a bank account but not always a POS

\- Speaks English and Setswana

\- Trust is local and relationship-based



\*\*Pains:\*\*



\- No digital presence beyond a Facebook page

\- Cannot be found by international guests except through intermediaries

\- Loses 25–35% margin to intermediaries (Booking.com, Viator, Expedia)

\- Cannot easily manage availability or rates across channels

\- Cash-flow constrained; cannot afford expensive PMS

\- Cannot afford to build or maintain a website

\- No way to accept international payments

\- No way to reach out to past guests

\- No visibility into who is booking, when, or why



\*\*Desired outcomes:\*\*



\- More bookings, direct or through the platform

\- Higher margin on each booking

\- Less admin work, especially on confirmations

\- Predictable payouts

\- Visibility into performance

\- Simple tools (WhatsApp-first)

\- Support in Setswana and English



\*\*How ArriveSuite addresses this persona:\*\*



\- \*\*Extranet (light PMS)\*\* — free tier, WhatsApp-native, works on any phone

\- \*\*WhatsApp integration\*\* — suppliers confirm bookings, update availability, receive payouts via WhatsApp

\- \*\*Field onboarding\*\* — ArriveSuite staff visit the lodge, set up the account, take photos, enter rates

\- \*\*Content production\*\* — ArriveSuite produces photos, descriptions, itineraries for the supplier

\- \*\*Multi-channel distribution\*\* — the same listing goes to B2C, B2B, corporate, WhatsApp, and OTA partners

\- \*\*Weekly payouts\*\* — same day, BWP native, no FX exposure for the supplier

\- \*\*Performance visibility\*\* — simple dashboard showing bookings, revenue, ratings

\- \*\*WhatsApp support\*\* — a Botswana-based support team reachable on WhatsApp



\*\*What this persona will never accept:\*\*



\- A complex web-only interface

\- English-only

\- Hidden fees

\- Slow payouts

\- Being locked into a system they cannot leave



\*\*Key metrics for this persona:\*\*



\- Time from first contact to first booking received

\- Bookings received per month

\- Average revenue per booking

\- Payout timeliness

\- Retention rate

\# §4 System Architecture



\## 4.1 Architectural Principles



ArriveSuite's architecture is built on seven principles:



1\. \*\*Hybrid schema.\*\* Domain-specific inventory tables (owned) + canonical envelope for bookings. No polymorphic JSON for storage.

2\. \*\*Two inventory engines, one booking layer.\*\* Owned inventory is relational. Sourced inventory is cached and thin. Bookings unify both.

3\. \*\*Server-side authority.\*\* Pricing, quotes, availability, and booking state are computed server-side. The client displays; it never determines.

4\. \*\*Idempotency everywhere.\*\* Every write accepts and honors an idempotency key.

5\. \*\*Adapter pattern.\*\* Every external supplier, payment provider, messaging channel, and notification service is behind an interface.

6\. \*\*Event-driven workflows.\*\* Every state transition emits an event. Every event is audited.

7\. \*\*RLS at the DB layer.\*\* Suppliers, agents, and corporate users see only their own data.



\## 4.2 High-Level Architecture



ArriveSuite has three layers:



\*\*Demand side:\*\* B2C web, B2B portal, Corporate portal, Supplier extranet — all accessed directly or via WhatsApp Business API (the primary interface for Botswana guests and suppliers).



\*\*Platform engine:\*\* Search (PostGIS, multi-inventory), Pricing Engine (signed quotes), Booking Workflows (state machines), Payments (PayTabs + Flutterwave), CRM, Support (threads, WhatsApp, email), Workflow Engine (BullMQ), Ledger (double-entry), Audit (immutable, 7 years).



\*\*Supply side:\*\*

\- Owned inventory: Stays, Tours, Activities, MICE (via extranet)

\- Sourced inventory: Flights (Duffel), Hotels (Amadeus), Tours (Viator — Phase 3), Cars (Rentalcars — Phase 3)

\- Consolidated inventory (Phase 4): Partner DMCs (Namibia, Zambia, Zim)



\*\*Exchange layer (Phase 6):\*\* Outbound REST API, XML-out, webhooks. Consumers: OTAs (Viator, Booking.com, Expedia), GDS, agents. Inbound: Partner DMC inventory, feed ingestion.



\## 4.3 Request Flow — B2C Booking (Sourced)

Guest searches on web or WhatsApp

→ Search API (multi-inventory)

→ Results merged, deduped, ranked

→ Guest selects product

→ Pricing Engine resolves net + markup + tax + FX (server-side)

→ Signed quote issued with 15-min expiry

→ Guest enters details

→ Booking created (draft → pending\_payment)

→ Workflow instance started

→ Payment intent created (PayTabs)

→ Guest redirected to PayTabs

→ PayTabs webhook → Payment captured

→ Workflow → ticketing

→ Supplier API called (Duffel order)

→ Workflow → ticketed

→ Voucher PDF generated → stored in R2

→ WhatsApp notification sent

→ Ledger entries posted

→ Reconciliation scheduled





\## 4.4 Request Flow — Supplier Onboarding



Supplier applies via web or WhatsApp

→ Onboarding workflow started

→ KYC collected (legal name, BTO license, tax ID, bank details)

→ Documents uploaded (R2)

→ Supplier Ops review

→ Approve → contract sent

→ Contract signed (e-sign)

→ Extranet account activated

→ Supplier onboards properties/tours

→ Content production (with ArriveSuite support)

→ Listing published

→ Appears in search (owned inventory)

→ Trust score begins accumulating





\## 4.5 Request Flow — WhatsApp Booking



Guest sends WhatsApp message

→ Meta Cloud API webhook → webhook handler

→ Signature verified

→ Routed to conversation handler

→ Simple command OR LLM parsing

→ Search performed

→ Results formatted as WhatsApp message

→ Guest selects → signed quote generated

→ Payment link sent (signed, 15-min expiry)

→ Guest pays via PayTabs hosted page

→ Webhook → Booking confirmed

→ Voucher PDF sent via WhatsApp

→ Messages logged to message\_threads

→ Audit events written





\## 4.6 Request Flow — Supplier WhatsApp Ops



Supplier sends WhatsApp message

→ Signature verified, phone looked up

→ Supplier identified

→ Message parsed by LLM

→ Intent determined (confirm, update, etc.)

→ Confirmation sent back

→ Supplier confirms → change applied

→ Audit events written

→ Downstream systems notified

\## 4.8 Data Flow — Multi-Inventory Merge



When a guest searches for stays in Maun:



1\. \*\*Search request\*\* hits the API with `q=Maun`, dates, pax

2\. \*\*Three parallel lookups:\*\*

&#x20;  - Owned inventory (Postgres + PostGIS)

&#x20;  - Sourced inventory (Duffel/Amadeus + Redis cache)

&#x20;  - Consolidated inventory (Phase 4)

3\. \*\*Merge \& dedupe engine:\*\*

&#x20;  - Same property detected across sources

&#x20;  - Cheapest supplier shown

&#x20;  - Trust signal applied

&#x20;  - Owned prioritized when prices comparable (±10%)

4\. \*\*Ranking engine:\*\*

&#x20;  - Price, trust, owned boost, rating

5\. \*\*Results returned:\*\*

&#x20;  - "Botswana Verified" for owned

&#x20;  - "Partner availability" for sourced



\## 4.9 Security Architecture



\- \*\*Edge (Vercel/Cloudflare):\*\* TLS, DDoS protection, WAF, rate limiting

\- \*\*App layer:\*\* Auth (Better-Auth), RBAC, CSRF, Zod validation, CORS allowlist, idempotency keys

\- \*\*Data layer:\*\* RLS, AES-256-GCM for PII and credentials, encrypted at rest and in transit, immutable audit\_events

\- \*\*Integration layer:\*\* Webhook signature verification, encrypted credentials, circuit breakers, payload redaction



\## 4.10 Reliability Architecture



| Concern | Mechanism |

|---|---|

| Availability | Multi-region (Phase 3+), graceful degradation |

| Circuit breakers | Per-provider, 5 failures in 60s opens for 30s |

| Retries | Exponential backoff, max 5 attempts |

| Idempotency | Client-generated keys, server dedupe, 24h retention |

| Reconciliation | 15 min inventory, 5 min payments, hourly FX |

| Backups | Daily snapshots + WAL for point-in-time recovery |

| Failover | P1 contracts auto-failover to declared target |

| Webhooks | Signature-verified, retried, dead-letter queue |

| Timeouts | Every external call, default 10s |

\## 4.13 Integration Architecture



Every external integration follows the same pattern:



Business Logic

→ Adapter Interface (TypeScript)

→ Provider Implementation

├─► Idempotency check

├─► Rate limit check

├─► Circuit breaker check

├─► Sign request

├─► HTTP call (with timeout)

├─► Verify response signature

├─► Normalize response

├─► Log to integration\_payloads

└─► Return normalized result
